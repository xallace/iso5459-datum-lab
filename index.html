PLATE III · COMMON DATUM |A-B| (DUAL BEARING SHAFT) VS. SINGLE DATUM |A|

Common Datum | A-B |

A rotating drive shaft is supported by two bearings (A and B), not one! Specifying runout relative to a single short bearing seat | A | extrapolates tiny bearing tilt across the entire shaft length — causing massive false rejects at the pinion. Connecting both bearing centers with a hyphen | A-B | establishes the true functional rotation axis.

Datum Targets (A1, A2, A3)

Raw sand castings, forged brackets, and welded frames have wavy, uneven surfaces. Calling the entire rough face Datum A creates a rocking, unrepeatable inspection setup. Under DIN EN ISO 5459, specify three explicit Datum Target Areas (Ø6 / A1, A2, A3) so every fixture and CMM seats on the exact same 3 points.

GOLDEN RULES OF ISO 5459
  • Mirror real assembly sequence: Primary Datum A is the largest functional mounting face the part sits on first — never a tiny cosmetic chamfer.
  • Long edge before short edge: Make the longer functional guide edge Secondary (2 points) to minimize angular lever-arm amplification.
  • Use Common Datum A-B whenever a shaft is carried by two spaced bearings.
  • Use Datum Targets (A1, A2, A3) on unmachined castings, forgings, and weldments.
FREQUENT DRAWING ERRORS
  • Forbidden letters (ISO 5459 §7.2.2): Never use I, O, Q, or X as datum identifiers (confused with 1, 0, Ø, or multiplication).
  • CAD-sketch datums: Dimensioning from arbitrary CAD origin planes instead of physical mating features inflates manufacturing cost.
  • Swapping Secondary & Tertiary casually: |A|B|C| and |A|C|B| define completely different coordinate frames on imperfect real parts.
